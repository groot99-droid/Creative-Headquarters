### 2.A Grid Geometry & Structural Frameworks
**Category Overview:** Underlying structural systems governing layout organization, alignment, and proportion that produce coherent, ordered, and rhythmically consistent compositional frameworks across media.

*   **2.A.1 Column & Modular Grids:** Vertical columns and modular unit cells organize content; column count and module proportion structure alignment and flexible layout arrangement.
*   **2.A.2 Baseline Grid:** Horizontal rhythm aligning text to consistent vertical increment; baseline grid unifies multi-column type alignment and vertical rhythm.
*   **2.A.3 Margins & Gutters:** Outer margin framing and inter-column gutter spacing; proportional margins establish breathing room and prevent content-edge crowding.
*   **2.A.4 Golden Section Layout:** Phi-ratio proportional division for harmonious page geometry; classical canon guides margin and text-block placement historically.
*   **2.A.5 Rule of Thirds & Focal Zones:** Dividing layout into ninths; intersection placement of focal elements creates dynamic, balanced compositional interest.
*   **2.A.6 Manuscript & Symmetrical Grid:** Single-column text block with symmetric margins; classical book layout prioritizing readability and traditional proportional harmony.
*   **2.A.7 Compound & Hierarchical Grids:** Overlapping multiple grid systems accommodating varied content types; compound grids enable complex, flexible, multi-zone editorial layouts.
*   **2.A.8 Breaking the Grid:** Intentional element placement violating grid for emphasis, tension, or dynamism; effective rule-breaking requires established grid foundation first.
*   **2.A.9 Responsive Grid Reflow:** Fluid grid adapting column count and arrangement across viewport sizes; breakpoints reorganize layout for optimal cross-device presentation.
*   **2.A.10 White Space & Negative Structure:** Intentional emptiness as active design element; white space improves readability, focus, hierarchy, and perceived sophistication significantly.

### 2.B Micro-Typography, Kerning, & Glyphic Anatomy
**Category Overview:** Detail-level typographic craft governing letterform spacing, adjustment, and refinement that produces polished, readable, professionally-tuned text at the character and line level.

*   **2.B.1 Glyph Anatomy Terms:** Stem, bowl, counter, serif, ascender, descender, terminal; anatomical vocabulary describes letterform components for precise typographic discussion.
*   **2.B.2 Kerning & Pair Adjustment:** Adjusting space between specific problem letter pairs (AV, To); kerning corrects uneven optical spacing for even rhythm.
*   **2.B.3 Tracking & Letterspacing:** Uniform spacing adjustment across character range; tracking loosens/tightens overall text; caps and small text benefit from added tracking.
*   **2.B.4 Leading & Line Height:** Vertical space between baselines; optimal leading (typically 120-145% of size) balances readability against text-block density.
*   **2.B.5 Ligatures & Alternates:** Combined glyphs (fi, fl) preventing collision; contextual and stylistic alternates enhance aesthetics and resolve awkward letter combinations.
*   **2.B.6 Hyphenation & Justification:** Balancing word spacing in justified text; hyphenation prevents rivers and excessive gaps while maintaining even gray text density.
*   **2.B.7 Widows & Orphans:** Isolated single lines at paragraph/column start or end; eliminating widows/orphans preserves clean, professional text-block appearance.
*   **2.B.8 Optical Alignment:** Adjusting punctuation and round characters to appear aligned; optical margin alignment corrects mechanical alignment's visual imbalance.
*   **2.B.9 x-Height & Cap Height:** Lowercase and capital vertical proportions; large x-height improves small-size legibility; proportions affect apparent size and pairing.
*   **2.B.10 Rag & Rhythm Control:** Shaping ragged-edge line endings for smooth, even contour; controlling rag avoids awkward shapes and improves reading rhythm.

### 2.C Visual Hierarchy Scaling & Reading Paths
**Category Overview:** Attention-direction systems governing element prominence, sequence, and scanning that guide viewer eye through content in intended priority and reading order.

*   **2.C.1 Type Scale & Modular Ratio:** Proportional size relationships (1.25, 1.618 ratios) between hierarchy levels; consistent scale creates harmonious, clear typographic size hierarchy.
*   **2.C.2 Weight & Contrast Hierarchy:** Bold, regular, light weight differentiation establishes importance; weight contrast signals hierarchy more subtly than size alone.
*   **2.C.3 Reading Gravity & Z-Pattern:** Western eyes enter top-left, scan in Z; layout leverages natural reading path for content sequence priority.
*   **2.C.4 F-Pattern Scanning:** Text-heavy content scanned in F-shape (horizontal top, vertical left); front-loading key information matches scanning behavior.
*   **2.C.5 Emphasis Techniques:** Bold, italic, color, size, and isolation signal importance; restrained emphasis preserves impact; over-emphasis dilutes hierarchy entirely.
*   **2.C.6 Grouping & Proximity:** Related elements clustered together read as unit; Gestalt proximity organizes information into digestible perceptual groups.
*   **2.C.7 Size & Position Priority:** Larger, higher, and centered elements read as most important; size and placement encode relative content significance.
*   **2.C.8 Color & Value Hierarchy:** High-contrast and saturated elements advance and attract; color and value differentiation reinforce importance and reading sequence.
*   **2.C.9 Entry Point & Flow:** Establishing clear starting focal point then guiding eye movement; deliberate entry and flow control reading experience.
*   **2.C.10 Information Layering:** Structuring content in scannable tiers (headline, subhead, body); layering serves both quick scanning and deep reading.

### 2.D Brand Psychology & Chromatic Identity
**Category Overview:** Brand-identity systems governing color psychology, mark design, and consistent identity expression that build recognition, emotional resonance, and coherent brand perception.

*   **2.D.1 Color Psychology Associations:** Hue-emotion mappings (blue=trust, red=urgency, green=growth); psychological color associations inform brand palette strategic selection.
*   **2.D.2 Brand Palette Systems:** Primary, secondary, and accent color hierarchy with defined values; systematic palette ensures flexible yet consistent brand application.
*   **2.D.3 Logo & Mark Design:** Memorable, scalable, versatile identity mark; effective logos work in monochrome, small size, and across diverse application contexts.
*   **2.D.4 Brand Voice & Tone:** Consistent verbal personality across communications; typographic and copy voice reinforce brand character and audience relationship consistently.
*   **2.D.5 Consistency & Guidelines:** Documented usage rules for logo, color, type, spacing; brand guidelines ensure coherent application across teams and touchpoints.
*   **2.D.6 Cultural Color Semiotics:** Color meanings vary across cultures (white=purity/mourning); global brands account for cultural color interpretation differences carefully.
*   **2.D.7 Contrast & Accessibility Ratios:** Brand colors meeting WCAG contrast minimums; accessible palettes ensure legibility while preserving brand identity and recognition.
*   **2.D.8 Emotional Resonance Mapping:** Aligning visual identity with target emotional response; deliberate aesthetic choices evoke intended feeling and brand association.
*   **2.D.9 Recognition & Recall:** Distinctive, consistent identity elements build memory; repetition and uniqueness drive brand recognition and unaided recall over time.
*   **2.D.10 Identity System Scalability:** Flexible identity adapting across formats and sizes; scalable systems maintain recognition from favicon to billboard consistently.

### 2.E Advertising Design Systems & Layout Theory
**Category Overview:** Advertising composition principles governing persuasive layout, message hierarchy, and campaign consistency that maximize attention, comprehension, and conversion across formats.

*   **2.E.1 AIDA & Persuasion Structure:** Attention, Interest, Desire, Action sequence organizes persuasive communication; layout guides viewer through psychological conversion funnel stages.
*   **2.E.2 Headline & Body Hierarchy:** Dominant headline captures attention, subordinate body elaborates; clear headline-body hierarchy delivers message in scannable priority order.
*   **2.E.3 Focal Image & Hero Shot:** Single dominant visual anchors composition and communicates instantly; hero image carries emotional and informational advertising weight primarily.
*   **2.E.4 Call-to-Action Placement:** Prominent, contrasting CTA positioned in natural eye path; clear action prompt at decision moment drives conversion.
*   **2.E.5 Campaign Consistency:** Unified visual system across ad variations and channels; consistency builds recognition and reinforces message through repeated exposure.
*   **2.E.6 Copy-Visual Integration:** Text and image working synergistically, not redundantly; integrated copy-visual relationship amplifies message beyond either element alone.
*   **2.E.7 Format & Media Adaptation:** Adapting core creative across billboard, print, digital, social; format-appropriate adaptation preserves message while respecting medium constraints.
*   **2.E.8 Balance & Tension Layout:** Symmetrical balance conveys stability; asymmetrical tension creates dynamism; balance choice matches brand tone and attention goal.
*   **2.E.9 Rule of Odds & Grouping:** Odd-numbered element groupings feel dynamic and natural; grouping and number affect compositional interest and visual comfort.
*   **2.E.10 A/B Layout Testing:** Comparative testing of layout variants against conversion metrics; data-driven iteration optimizes advertising effectiveness empirically over intuition.

### 2.G Print Production, Inks, & Material Finishes
**Category Overview:** Physical production systems governing color reproduction, material selection, and finishing that translate digital design into accurate, high-quality tangible printed output.

*   **2.G.1 CMYK vs Spot Color:** Four-color process simulates spectrum; spot (Pantone) inks ensure precise consistent color; each suits different reproduction needs.
*   **2.G.2 Bleed, Trim, & Safe Zone:** Bleed extends art past trim edge; safe zone keeps content clear of cut; margins prevent trimming errors.
*   **2.G.3 Resolution & DPI:** 300 DPI standard for print image sharpness; insufficient resolution produces pixelation; vector art scales without resolution dependency.
*   **2.G.4 Paper Stock & Weight:** Paper weight (GSM), finish (matte, gloss), and texture affect perception; stock selection shapes tactile and visual quality.
*   **2.G.5 Offset vs Digital Printing:** Offset excels at high-volume consistency; digital enables short-run and variable data; method matches quantity and requirement.
*   **2.G.6 Special Finishes (Foil/Emboss):** Foil stamping, embossing, spot UV, and die-cut add tactile premium distinction; special finishes elevate perceived value.
*   **2.G.7 Overprint & Trapping:** Overprint layers ink; trapping overlaps colors preventing registration-gap white slivers; prepress trapping ensures clean color boundary reproduction.
*   **2.G.8 Color Proofing & Calibration:** Hard/soft proofs verify color before production; calibrated workflow ensures screen-to-print color accuracy and predictable results.
*   **2.G.9 Binding & Folding:** Saddle-stitch, perfect bind, and fold types affect function and cost; binding selection matches page count and use.
*   **2.G.10 Prepress & File Prep:** Preflight checks, correct color mode, embedded fonts, and proper bleed; thorough prepress prevents costly production errors downstream.

### 2.H Digital Interface Ergonomics & Responsive Systems
**Category Overview:** Interface-design systems governing usability, responsiveness, and interaction ergonomics that produce efficient, accessible, cross-device digital experiences respecting human cognitive and physical constraints.

*   **2.H.1 Responsive Breakpoints:** Layout adaptation thresholds across device widths; breakpoints reorganize content for optimal mobile, tablet, and desktop presentation.
*   **2.H.2 Touch Target Sizing:** Minimum 44-48px interactive targets for finger accuracy; adequate touch-target size and spacing prevents mis-taps on touchscreens.
*   **2.H.3 Fitts's & Hick's Law:** Target acquisition time relates to size/distance (Fitts); choice time increases with options (Hick); laws guide interface efficiency.
*   **2.H.4 Component & Design Systems:** Reusable UI component libraries with defined variants; design systems ensure consistency, efficiency, and scalability across products.
*   **2.H.5 Fluid Type & Spacing:** Viewport-relative scaling of type and spacing; fluid systems adapt smoothly across screen sizes without fixed breakpoints.
*   **2.H.6 Visual Feedback & States:** Hover, active, focus, disabled, and loading states communicate interactivity; clear feedback confirms user action and system status.
*   **2.H.7 Navigation Patterns:** Menu, tab, breadcrumb, and hamburger conventions; familiar navigation patterns leverage learned behavior reducing cognitive orientation cost.
*   **2.H.8 Cognitive Load Reduction:** Minimizing simultaneous decisions and information; progressive disclosure, chunking, and clarity reduce mental effort and decision fatigue.
*   **2.H.9 Cross-Device Consistency:** Coherent experience across platforms while respecting device conventions; consistency aids learning, adaptation respects platform-specific interaction norms.
*   **2.H.10 Progressive Disclosure:** Revealing information incrementally as needed; hiding complexity behind layers prevents overwhelming users while preserving feature accessibility.

### 2.I History of Type Design & Stylistic Movements
**Category Overview:** Typographic heritage systems governing typeface classification, historical evolution, and stylistic lineage that inform authentic, contextually-appropriate, and knowledgeable type selection and pairing.

*   **2.I.1 Serif Classification:** Old-style, transitional, modern (Didone), slab serif categories; serif classification reflects historical period, stroke contrast, and bracket style.
*   **2.I.2 Sans-Serif Evolution:** Grotesque, neo-grotesque, humanist, geometric sans lineages; sans-serif development reflects modernist ideals and functional legibility priorities.
*   **2.I.3 Type Anatomy Lineage:** Letterform features tracing calligraphic and engraving origins; understanding historical construction informs authentic type usage and appreciation.
*   **2.I.4 Movement Aesthetics:** Design movements (Bauhaus, Swiss, psychedelic, grunge) each carry distinct typographic sensibility, grid attitude, and expressive philosophy.
*   **2.I.5 Metal to Digital Transition:** Evolution from hot-metal to phototypesetting to digital; technology shifts reshaped type design, spacing, and reproduction possibility.
*   **2.I.6 Display vs Text Faces:** Display faces optimized for large size and impact; text faces optimized for extended small-size reading comfort and legibility.
*   **2.I.7 Revival & Reinterpretation:** Digital revivals of historical typefaces balance fidelity and modern function; revivals preserve heritage while adapting for contemporary use.
*   **2.I.8 Cultural Type Traditions:** Non-Latin scripts (CJK, Arabic, Devanagari) carry distinct construction, direction, and aesthetic; global typography respects cultural conventions.
*   **2.I.9 Variable Font Technology:** Single font file containing continuous weight, width, optical axes; variable fonts enable flexible, efficient, responsive typographic systems.
*   **2.I.10 Typeface Pairing Logic:** Combining contrasting yet harmonious faces (serif+sans); effective pairing balances contrast, mood, and functional hierarchy differentiation.

### 2.J WCAG Accessibility Standards & Legibility Physics
**Category Overview:** Accessibility-compliance systems governing contrast, sizing, and inclusive design that ensure content is perceivable, operable, and understandable for users with diverse abilities.

*   **2.J.1 Contrast Ratio Thresholds:** WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large; contrast ratios ensure text legibility for low-vision users.
*   **2.J.2 Minimum Type Size:** Adequate base font size (16px+ body) and scalability; sufficient size supports readability across visual ability ranges.
*   **2.J.3 Color-Blind Safe Palettes:** Avoiding color-only information encoding; palettes distinguishable by deuteranopia/protanopia users; redundant cues supplement color differentiation always.
*   **2.J.4 Screen Reader Semantics:** Semantic HTML structure and ARIA labels; proper markup enables assistive technology to convey content and interface meaning.
*   **2.J.5 Focus & Keyboard Navigation:** Visible focus indicators and logical tab order; full keyboard operability serves users unable to use pointing devices.
*   **2.J.6 Text Spacing Requirements:** Adjustable line-height, letter, word, and paragraph spacing; WCAG spacing minimums support dyslexic and low-vision reading needs.
*   **2.J.7 Alt Text & Descriptions:** Descriptive alternative text for images; alt text conveys visual content meaning to screen-reader and low-bandwidth users.
*   **2.J.8 Motion & Animation Safety:** Respecting reduced-motion preferences; limiting flashing (seizure risk) and providing pause controls protects vestibular-sensitive and photosensitive users.
*   **2.J.9 Readability Metrics:** Reading-level scoring, line length (45-75 characters), and clear language; measurable readability supports comprehension across literacy levels.
*   **2.J.10 Reflow & Zoom Support:** Content reflowing without horizontal scroll at 400% zoom; reflow and zoom support serve low-vision magnification users properly.
