## 2. DEEP RESEARCH (The Expert Knowledge Base)
*System Note: Elite, industry-standard ground truths in compressed technical shorthand (15-20 words/node).*

### 2.A Global Macro-Narrative Structural Frameworks
**Category Overview:** Large-scale story architecture templates governing sequencing of setup, escalation, and resolution across cultures, mediums, and non-linear structural experimentation.

*   **2.A.1 Three-Act Structure:** Setup-confrontation-resolution with plot points at ~25%/75% marks; inciting incident, midpoint pivot, climax, denouement organize dramatic arc.
*   **2.A.2 Hero's Journey (Monomyth):** Campbell's departure-initiation-return cycle; call, threshold, ordeal, reward, road-back stages map transformational protagonist arc across genres.
*   **2.A.3 Five-Act Freytag Pyramid:** Exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, catastrophe/resolution; classical dramatic structure emphasizing peak-and-descent tension geometry.
*   **2.A.4 Save the Cat Beat Sheet:** Fifteen-beat commercial screenplay template with precise page-percentage placement for opening image, catalyst, break-into-two, midpoint, finale.
*   **2.A.5 Nonlinear/Fractured Timeline:** Chronology deliberately disordered via flashback, flash-forward, or reverse sequence to build mystery, thematic resonance, or dramatic recontextualization.
*   **2.A.6 Kishōtenketsu:** Four-act East Asian structure (introduction, development, twist, reconciliation) driven by juxtaposition rather than conflict-based Western escalation logic.
*   **2.A.7 Frame Narrative:** Story-within-story nesting where outer frame contextualizes inner tale, adding layered perspective, unreliability, or thematic mirroring between levels.
*   **2.A.8 In Medias Res:** Opening mid-action before providing context, hooking reader immediately, then filling backstory through delayed exposition and retrospective reveal.
*   **2.A.9 Braided/Multi-POV Structure:** Multiple parallel narrative threads or viewpoints interwoven, converging toward shared climax or thematic unity across distinct perspectives.
*   **2.A.10 Circular/Recursive Form:** Ending returns to opening image or situation, transformed by intervening events, creating structural closure and thematic recontextualization.

### 2.B Micro-Scene Architecture & Structural Beats
**Category Overview:** Scene-level construction principles governing goal, conflict, and value shift that make individual units propulsive, purposeful, and structurally load-bearing.

*   **2.B.1 Scene Goal & Conflict:** POV character enters with objective; opposition creates conflict; scene dramatizes struggle toward or away from that goal.
*   **2.B.2 Value Charge Shift:** Every functional scene must shift a story value (life/death, love/hate, hope/despair) from positive to negative or reverse.
*   **2.B.3 Beat/Action Units:** Smallest exchange of behavior/reaction; beats accumulate into scene rhythm, each shifting momentum or revealing character incrementally.
*   **2.B.4 Scene-Sequel Pairing:** Proactive scene (goal-conflict-disaster) followed by reactive sequel (reaction-dilemma-decision), alternating action and processing for pacing rhythm.
*   **2.B.5 Turning Point & Reversal:** Mid-scene event inverts expectation or fortune, preventing stasis and driving narrative into unanticipated consequence or complication.
*   **2.B.6 Entry & Exit Hooks:** Scenes begin late (after unnecessary setup) and end early (on unresolved tension) to maximize propulsion and reader momentum.
*   **2.B.7 Setting as Pressure:** Environment weaponized to constrain, threaten, or complicate character objective, transforming passive backdrop into active dramatic force.
*   **2.B.8 Micro-Tension Threading:** Sentence-level unease, contradiction, or anticipation sustains reader engagement even within low-stakes or transitional scene passages.
*   **2.B.9 Time Compression & Expansion:** Selectively dilating significant moments and compressing insignificant duration controls scene tempo and directs reader emotional attention.
*   **2.B.10 Scene Purpose Audit:** Each scene must advance plot, reveal character, or deepen theme (ideally multiple); purposeless scenes cut in revision.

### 2.C Character Psychology & Voice Profiling
**Category Overview:** Interior architecture systems governing motivation, contradiction, and distinctive voice that produce believable, dimensional, and dramatically compelling characters.

*   **2.C.1 Want vs Need:** External conscious desire (want) contrasts internal unconscious requirement (need); arc reconciles or tragically fails to reconcile them.
*   **2.C.2 Wound & Backstory:** Formative past trauma shapes present behavior, fear, and defensive worldview, providing psychological causality behind character choices and resistance.
*   **2.C.3 Ghost & Lie:** Haunting past event (ghost) generates false belief (lie) character holds about themselves/world, which arc challenges and dismantles.
*   **2.C.4 Moral Complexity:** Multidimensional characters combine virtue and flaw; sympathetic antagonists and flawed protagonists resist binary good/evil categorization for realism.
*   **2.C.5 Idiolect & Speech Signature:** Distinctive vocabulary, syntax rhythm, verbal tics, and diction level make each character's voice instantly identifiable without attribution.
*   **2.C.6 Arc Types:** Positive change, negative fall, flat/testing arcs; character either transforms, corrupts, or steadfastly changes surrounding world instead.
*   **2.C.7 Interiority & Consciousness:** Access to character thought, sensation, and psychological process via free indirect discourse, stream-of-consciousness, or filtered narrative distance.
*   **2.C.8 Contradiction & Flaw:** Internal inconsistency (brave yet insecure) and specific weakness create dimensional realism, relatability, and dramatic vulnerability points.
*   **2.C.9 Agency & Decision:** Characters drive plot through active choice under pressure rather than passive reaction; meaningful decisions reveal true values and priorities.
*   **2.C.10 Foil & Contrast Mapping:** Secondary characters designed to highlight protagonist traits through opposition or parallel, illuminating theme via comparative juxtaposition.

### 2.D Dialogue Mechanics & Subtextual Layering
**Category Overview:** Spoken-word craft governing subtext, rhythm, and characterization that makes dialogue dramatically functional, distinctive, and layered beneath surface content.

*   **2.D.1 Subtext & Withholding:** Characters rarely state true feeling directly; meaning conveyed beneath literal words through evasion, deflection, and unspoken tension.
*   **2.D.2 Beat Attribution & Action Tags:** Interspersed physical action replaces repetitive "said" tags, grounding dialogue in scene while conveying character state and pacing.
*   **2.D.3 On-the-Nose Avoidance:** Eliminating dialogue that explicitly states exactly what characters feel/mean; obliqueness and indirection create realistic, engaging exchange.
*   **2.D.4 Rhythm & Interruption:** Overlapping speech, incomplete sentences, and interruption patterns replicate authentic conversational cadence and escalating emotional intensity.
*   **2.D.5 Register & Diction Shift:** Character adjusts formality, vocabulary, and syntax based on relationship, status, and situation, revealing social dynamics and code-switching.
*   **2.D.6 Conflict Escalation:** Dialogue exchanges progressively raise stakes, tension, or emotional temperature rather than remaining static informational transfer between characters.
*   **2.D.7 Exposition Concealment:** Necessary information embedded within argument, banter, or conflict so audience absorbs backstory without noticing expository intent.
*   **2.D.8 Silence & Pause:** Strategic non-response, ellipsis, and beat pauses carry dramatic weight, discomfort, or unspoken meaning exceeding verbal content.
*   **2.D.9 Distinctive Cadence:** Each character's sentence length, rhythm, and pause pattern forms recognizable auditory signature independent of vocabulary content.
*   **2.D.10 Power Dynamics in Speech:** Who speaks, interrupts, questions, or controls conversational direction encodes and reveals hierarchical relationship and shifting status between characters.

### 2.E Pacing, Cadence, & Prose Syntax Dynamics
**Category Overview:** Sentence and paragraph-level rhythmic engineering controlling reading tempo, momentum, and prosodic musicality across scene intensity and structural variation.

*   **2.E.1 Sentence Length Variation:** Short sentences accelerate and punch; long sentences create flow or breathlessness; deliberate variation prevents monotony and controls tempo.
*   **2.E.2 Paragraph Rhythm & Break:** Paragraph length and break placement control pacing beats; single-line paragraphs emphasize, dense blocks slow, whitespace breathes.
*   **2.E.3 White Space & Density:** Page visual density affects reading speed and perceived intensity; sparse whitespace accelerates, dense text slows and immerses.
*   **2.E.4 Scene vs Summary Balance:** Dramatized real-time scene vs compressed narrative summary; ratio controls pacing, with scene for significance and summary for transition.
*   **2.E.5 Tension Modulation Curve:** Alternating high-intensity and quieter recovery passages prevents reader fatigue while sustaining overall escalating dramatic momentum arc.
*   **2.E.6 Syntactic Parallelism:** Repeated grammatical structure across clauses/sentences creates rhythm, emphasis, and rhetorical power through patterned symmetry and cumulative force.
*   **2.E.7 Fragment & Run-On Intent:** Grammatically incomplete or extended sentences deliberately deployed for effect—fragments punch, run-ons rush—breaking rules purposefully for rhythm.
*   **2.E.8 Sound & Prosody:** Alliteration, assonance, consonance, and meter create auditory texture; prose musicality read aloud reveals rhythm and cadence quality.
*   **2.E.9 Momentum & Drag Control:** Identifying and excising passages that stall forward propulsion; maintaining narrative drive through consequence, question, and unresolved tension.
*   **2.E.10 Chapter/Section Break Placement:** Ending units on hooks, reveals, or cliffhangers propels continued reading; break rhythm controls macro-pacing experience.

### 2.F Analytical Formats, Rhetoric, & Dialectics
**Category Overview:** Argumentative and expository structures governing persuasion, logical rigor, and dialectical reasoning across essays, criticism, and analytical nonfiction discourse.

*   **2.F.1 Thesis-Argument-Evidence:** Central claim supported by logically sequenced arguments, each substantiated with concrete evidence, forming coherent persuasive analytical structure.
*   **2.F.2 Ethos/Pathos/Logos:** Aristotelian persuasion via credibility (ethos), emotional appeal (pathos), and logical reasoning (logos); balanced deployment maximizes rhetorical effectiveness.
*   **2.F.3 Dialectical Synthesis:** Thesis-antithesis-synthesis progression reconciles opposing positions into higher-order integrated understanding, transcending simple binary opposition through reasoned tension.
*   **2.F.4 Toulmin Model:** Claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal structure rigorously maps argument components, exposing hidden assumptions and logical connections.
*   **2.F.5 Counterargument & Rebuttal:** Anticipating and addressing opposing views strengthens argument credibility; concession followed by refutation demonstrates intellectual honesty and rigor.
*   **2.F.6 Syllogism & Deduction:** Major premise plus minor premise yields necessary conclusion; deductive validity depends on premise truth and logical form correctness.
*   **2.F.7 Inductive Reasoning:** Specific observations generalized into probable conclusions; strength depends on sample size, representativeness, and absence of confounding variables.
*   **2.F.8 Logical Fallacy Detection:** Identifying ad hominem, straw man, false dichotomy, circular reasoning, and correlation-causation errors that undermine argumentative validity.
*   **2.F.9 Comparative Framework:** Structured juxtaposition of subjects across consistent criteria, revealing similarities, differences, and evaluative judgment through systematic parallel analysis.
*   **2.F.10 Steelman & Charitable Reading:** Engaging opposing arguments in strongest possible form before refuting, demonstrating intellectual rigor and avoiding straw-man weakness.

### 2.G Expository Logic & World Ingestion Prose
**Category Overview:** Information-delivery craft governing how context, backstory, and world detail are conveyed without stalling narrative momentum or overwhelming reader cognition.

*   **2.G.1 Show vs Tell Calibration:** Dramatizing through sensory scene (show) vs efficient direct statement (tell); strategic balance based on significance and pacing need.
*   **2.G.2 Iceberg Withholding:** Author knows vastly more than revealed; withheld depth creates felt authority and mystery, surfacing only what narrative requires.
*   **2.G.3 Info-Dump Avoidance:** Preventing large undigested exposition blocks; distributing necessary information across scene, action, and dialogue in absorbable increments.
*   **2.G.4 Drip-Feed Revelation:** Metering out world/backstory information gradually as needed, sustaining curiosity and preventing cognitive overload while maintaining forward momentum.
*   **2.G.5 Diegetic Exposition:** Information conveyed through in-world mechanisms (documents, overheard conversation, environmental detail) rather than intrusive authorial narration.
*   **2.G.6 Contextual Grounding:** Orienting reader in time, place, and situation early enough to prevent confusion without front-loading excessive setup detail.
*   **2.G.7 Sensory Anchoring:** Concrete sensory detail (sight, sound, smell, texture) makes exposition vivid and immersive rather than abstract informational recitation.
*   **2.G.8 Concept Scaffolding:** Introducing complex ideas incrementally, building from familiar to novel, each layer supported by prior established understanding foundation.
*   **2.G.9 Reader Knowledge Modeling:** Tracking what audience already knows to avoid redundant explanation or confusing gaps; calibrating detail to assumed reader familiarity.
*   **2.G.10 Curiosity Gap Engineering:** Deliberately raising questions before answering, creating information asymmetry that compels continued reading to resolve narrative tension.

### 2.H Script Formatting Standards & Fountain Syntax
**Category Overview:** Screenplay and screenwriting formatting conventions governing industry-standard presentation, plain-text markup, and page-timing that ensure professional readability and production usability.

*   **2.H.1 Slugline & Scene Heading:** INT./EXT., location, time-of-day in caps establishes scene setting; consistent formatting enables production breakdown and scheduling.
*   **2.H.2 Action Line Convention:** Present-tense, concise visual description of on-screen action; capitalized character introductions and significant sound effects per standard practice.
*   **2.H.3 Character Cue & Dialogue:** Centered capitalized character name above dialogue block; consistent spacing and margin conventions per industry screenplay standard formatting.
*   **2.H.4 Parenthetical Usage:** Brief wryly/action note between cue and dialogue; used sparingly for essential delivery direction, avoiding over-direction of performance.
*   **2.H.5 Transitions:** CUT TO:, DISSOLVE TO:, FADE OUT. right-aligned scene transitions; modern convention minimizes explicit transitions favoring implied cuts.
*   **2.H.6 Fountain Markup Syntax:** Plain-text screenwriting format using simple markup (asterisks, forced elements) parseable into formatted screenplay across compatible software applications.
*   **2.H.7 Dual Dialogue:** Simultaneous overlapping speech formatted in parallel columns; Fountain uses caret notation to render concurrent character exchanges.
*   **2.H.8 Page-Per-Minute Rule:** One formatted screenplay page approximates one minute of screen time; standard for runtime estimation and pacing calibration.
*   **2.H.9 Shot & Camera Direction:** ANGLE ON, CLOSE ON, POV shot specifications; used judiciously as writers generally avoid excessive directorial camera prescription.
*   **2.H.10 Title Page & Metadata:** Title, author, contact, draft date formatted per convention; Fountain title-page key-value block encodes production metadata.

### 2.I Emotional Resonance & Narrative Anchoring
**Category Overview:** Affective engineering systems governing empathy, catharsis, and thematic anchoring that produce genuine emotional impact rather than manipulative or unearned sentiment.

*   **2.I.1 Empathy & Identification:** Reader emotional investment cultivated through vulnerability, relatable desire, and access to character interiority, enabling vicarious experiential connection.
*   **2.I.2 Stakes Escalation:** Progressively raising consequences (personal, relational, existential) intensifies reader investment; clear, meaningful stakes make outcomes matter emotionally.
*   **2.I.3 Catharsis Engineering:** Structured emotional release through climactic confrontation and resolution; buildup of pressure discharged for satisfying purgative audience experience.
*   **2.I.4 Dramatic Irony:** Audience knows what characters don't, generating tension, suspense, or poignancy through gap between reader knowledge and character awareness.
*   **2.I.5 Setup & Payoff:** Early planted detail (Chekhov's gun) rewarded through later significance; foreshadowing and callback create satisfying structural cohesion.
*   **2.I.6 Emotional Whiplash Control:** Managing transitions between contrasting emotional registers; earned tonal shifts vs jarring mood violations affecting reader trust and immersion.
*   **2.I.7 Universal Theme Anchor:** Grounding specific story in resonant universal human experience (loss, belonging, mortality) that transcends particular circumstance for broad connection.
*   **2.I.8 Vulnerability Beats:** Moments of character emotional exposure, weakness, or authentic feeling that deepen empathy and humanize otherwise guarded or competent figures.
*   **2.I.9 Sensory Emotional Memory:** Specific concrete sensory detail evokes visceral emotional association, anchoring abstract feeling in embodied, memorable, resonant particularity.
*   **2.I.10 Earned vs Cheap Emotion:** Emotional payoff justified by sufficient buildup and character investment vs manipulative sentimentality lacking dramatic groundwork and credibility.

### 2.J Editorial Iteration & Textual Optimization Protocols
**Category Overview:** Revision methodology systems governing structural, line-level, and voice-preserving refinement that transform draft material into polished, precise, professional final text.

*   **2.J.1 Line vs Developmental Edit:** Developmental addresses structure, character, plot; line edit refines prose, rhythm, word choice; distinct passes prevent premature polishing.
*   **2.J.2 Redundancy Excision:** Removing repeated information, duplicate description, and unnecessary reinforcement that dilutes impact and insults reader comprehension capacity.
*   **2.J.3 Filter Word Removal:** Eliminating "he saw/felt/noticed" distancing constructions that mediate experience; direct rendering increases immediacy and reader immersion.
*   **2.J.4 Active vs Passive Voice:** Preferring active construction for clarity, energy, and agency; passive reserved for deliberate emphasis, unknown agent, or rhythm.
*   **2.J.5 Clarity & Concision Pass:** Cutting unnecessary words, tightening sentences, and eliminating vagueness; every word must justify presence, maximizing density and precision.
*   **2.J.6 Consistency & Continuity Check:** Verifying character detail, timeline, spatial logic, and established fact consistency across manuscript to prevent immersion-breaking errors.
*   **2.J.7 Read-Aloud Cadence Test:** Vocalizing prose reveals awkward rhythm, tongue-twisters, unintended repetition, and cadence problems invisible during silent reading review.
*   **2.J.8 Kill-Your-Darlings Triage:** Removing beloved passages that don't serve the work; emotional attachment to writing must yield to structural and pacing necessity.
*   **2.J.9 Structural Reordering:** Rearranging scene, chapter, or argument sequence to optimize pacing, revelation timing, and logical or emotional flow effectiveness.
*   **2.J.10 Voice Preservation:** Maintaining author's distinctive stylistic signature through editing; refinement should sharpen rather than sanitize or homogenize characteristic voice.
