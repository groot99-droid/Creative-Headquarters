### 2.A Advanced Harmonic & Modal Theory
**Category Overview:** Structural pitch relationships governing tension, color, and tonal identity through chord function, modal borrowing, and non-diatonic voicing systems across genres.

*   **2.A.1 Modal Interchange & Borrowed Chords:** Chords borrowed from parallel major/minor mode inject unexpected color; bVI, bVII, iv commonly imported for emotional shading.
*   **2.A.2 Extended & Altered Voicings:** 9th, 11th, 13th extensions and altered tensions (b9, #11) add harmonic richness beyond triadic simplicity, jazz-derived color.
*   **2.A.3 Chromatic Mediants & Non-Functional Harmony:** Root movement by third outside diatonic function creates cinematic, unresolved harmonic color absent traditional cadential pull.
*   **2.A.4 Modes of the Major Scale:** Ionian through Locrian each carry distinct emotional character (Dorian=melancholic-hopeful, Phrygian=dark-exotic, Lydian=dreamlike-floating) via characteristic scale degree.
*   **2.A.5 Polytonality & Bitonal Layering:** Simultaneous independent key centers create dissonant, complex textural friction; common in modernist orchestral and experimental composition.
*   **2.A.6 Voice Leading & Counterpoint:** Smooth stepwise motion between chord tones minimizes leaps; species counterpoint rules govern independent melodic line interaction and consonance.
*   **2.A.7 Secondary Dominants & Tonicization:** V/V, V/vi temporarily tonicize non-tonic scale degrees, creating localized pull and harmonic momentum before resolving back home.
*   **2.A.8 Pedal Points & Drone Harmony:** Sustained bass or inner-voice tone under shifting harmony creates tension, grounding, and hypnotic stasis over changing chords.
*   **2.A.9 Quartal & Quintal Harmony:** Chords built in fourths/fifths rather than thirds produce ambiguous, open, non-functional sonorities common in modal jazz and impressionism.
*   **2.A.10 Microtonality & Just Intonation:** Pitches beyond 12-tone equal temperament; pure-ratio tuning systems create beatless consonance or unsettling non-Western intervallic textures.

### 2.B Rhythmic Structures & Time Signatures
**Category Overview:** Temporal organization systems governing pulse, subdivision, and metric grouping that establish groove identity, cross-cultural rhythmic language, and structural momentum.

*   **2.B.1 Simple vs Compound Meter:** Simple meter subdivides beats into two (4/4); compound meter subdivides into three (6/8), altering felt subdivision and lilt.
*   **2.B.2 Polyrhythm & Polymeter:** Simultaneous conflicting subdivisions (3-against-2, 4-against-3) or overlapping meters create rhythmic complexity and cross-pulse perceptual tension.
*   **2.B.3 Syncopation & Displacement:** Accenting weak beats or off-beats against expected pulse creates rhythmic surprise, groove tension, and forward propulsive momentum.
*   **2.B.4 Odd Time Signatures:** 5/4, 7/8, 11/8 asymmetric groupings (2+3, 3+2+2) create unsettled or exotic rhythmic feel departing from standard duple/triple meter.
*   **2.B.5 Groove Pocket & Swing Feel:** Micro-timing deviation from strict grid (behind/ahead beat placement) defines genre-specific feel; swing ratio alters eighth-note subdivision.
*   **2.B.6 Metric Modulation:** Tempo relationship shift via common subdivision reinterpretation smoothly transitions between differing time feels without perceived tempo discontinuity.
*   **2.B.7 Ostinato & Rhythmic Cells:** Repeated rhythmic-melodic fragment provides structural anchor beneath evolving harmonic or textural layers, common in minimalism and film scoring.
*   **2.B.8 Tempo Rubato:** Expressive elastic tempo fluctuation, stealing and returning time, prioritizes emotional phrasing over strict metronomic rhythmic adherence.
*   **2.B.9 Clave & Cross-Cultural Rhythm:** Foundational rhythmic key patterns (son clave, gahu, tresillo) organize polyrhythmic ensemble interaction across Afro-Latin and West African traditions.
*   **2.B.10 Rhythmic Density & Layering:** Stacking multiple rhythmic subdivisions (quarter, eighth, triplet) across instrument layers builds textural complexity and perceived rhythmic energy.

### 2.C Psychoacoustic Engineering
**Category Overview:** Human auditory perception mechanics governing how frequency, loudness, and timing cues are processed, informing mix decisions that exploit or avoid perceptual phenomena.

*   **2.C.1 Frequency Masking & Critical Bands:** Louder frequencies obscure nearby quieter ones within critical bandwidth; frequency-specific EQ carving prevents element masking in dense mixes.
*   **2.C.2 Haas Effect & Precedence:** Sounds arriving within 1-35ms perceived as single source from first-arrival direction; used for width creation without phase cancellation.
*   **2.C.3 Binaural Beats & Perception:** Slightly differing frequencies per ear create perceived phantom beat frequency, exploited for immersive or entrainment-focused ambient design.
*   **2.C.4 Fletcher-Munson Loudness:** Equal-loudness contours show human ears less sensitive to bass/treble at low volumes; mix translation checks required across playback levels.
*   **2.C.5 Transient Perception & Attack Time:** Fast attack transients convey punch and clarity; attack time perception under 10ms critical for percussive impact and definition.
*   **2.C.6 Spectral Balance & Tonal Masking:** Even energy distribution across frequency spectrum prevents listener fatigue; competing instruments in same range require complementary EQ carving.
*   **2.C.7 Auditory Streaming & Grouping:** Gestalt principles (similarity, proximity, continuity) determine how listeners parse simultaneous sound sources into distinct perceptual streams.
*   **2.C.8 Doppler Effect Simulation:** Pitch shift simulating relative source-listener velocity change creates convincing passing-by or approaching sound design for moving elements.
*   **2.C.9 Perceived Warmth & Saturation:** Harmonic distortion adding even-order overtones perceived as warm/pleasant; odd-order harmonics perceived as harsh/aggressive tonal character.
*   **2.C.10 Ear Fatigue & Listening Duration:** Excessive high-frequency energy, over-compression, or harsh transients accelerate listener fatigue; dynamic mix pacing preserves engagement over duration.

### 2.D Spatial Audio & Soundstage Geometry
**Category Overview:** Three-dimensional sound placement systems governing perceived width, depth, and elevation, translating mix decisions into immersive spatial listener experience across formats.

*   **2.D.1 Stereo Width & Panning Law:** Constant-power panning maintains perceived loudness across stereo field; width techniques (mid-side, haas) expand perceived stereo image.
*   **2.D.2 Binaural & HRTF Modeling:** Head-related transfer function simulates ear-shape filtering for convincing 3D headphone positioning without physical multi-speaker array.
*   **2.D.3 Ambisonics & 360 Audio:** Full-sphere surround capture/encoding format enables rotatable, format-agnostic spatial audio for VR and immersive multichannel delivery.
*   **2.D.4 Reverb Decay & Room Modeling:** RT60 decay time, early reflection pattern, and diffusion parameters simulate physical space size and material surface character.
*   **2.D.5 Object-Based Audio (Atmos):** Individual sound objects carry positional metadata rather than fixed channels, enabling dynamic renderer-based spatial placement per playback system.
*   **2.D.6 Depth Placement:** Near-field intimacy vs far-field distance conveyed through reverb ratio, high-frequency rolloff, and relative volume level cues.
*   **2.D.7 Surround Channel Architecture:** 5.1/7.1 discrete channel layouts assign specific speaker positions; bass management and phantom center affect translation across systems.
*   **2.D.8 Phase Coherence & Mono Compatibility:** Out-of-phase elements cancel when summed to mono; correlation meters verify translation integrity across mono playback systems.
*   **2.D.9 Early Reflections & Room Character:** First-arriving reflections within 50ms define perceived room size and material before diffuse late reverb tail develops.
*   **2.D.10 Elevation & Height Channels:** Overhead speaker layers (Atmos height channels) add vertical dimension, simulating rain, atmospheric texture, or overhead sonic events.

### 2.E Synthesis & Signal Processing Dynamics
**Category Overview:** Electronic sound generation and signal shaping methods governing timbre creation, modulation behavior, and dynamic control across synthesis architectures and processing chains.

*   **2.E.1 Subtractive Synthesis:** Harmonically rich waveform filtered via low/high/band-pass to sculpt timbre by removing frequency content from oscillator source material.
*   **2.E.2 FM/Phase Modulation:** Modulator oscillator frequency-modulates carrier, generating complex inharmonic sidebands; ratio and index control produce bell, metallic, or bass timbres.
*   **2.E.3 Wavetable & Granular Synthesis:** Wavetable morphs between stored waveform cycles; granular fragments audio into micro-grains for textural, time-stretched, or glitch synthesis.
*   **2.E.4 ADSR Envelope Shaping:** Attack-decay-sustain-release parameters shape amplitude/filter/pitch over time, defining percussive pluck versus sustained pad articulation character.
*   **2.E.5 LFO Modulation & Movement:** Low-frequency oscillator modulates pitch, filter, or amplitude cyclically, creating vibrato, tremolo, or evolving wobble movement over time.
*   **2.E.6 Compression & Dynamic Range Control:** Threshold, ratio, attack, release parameters reduce dynamic range, controlling peak-to-average level and perceived loudness consistency.
*   **2.E.7 EQ Curves & Frequency Sculpting:** Parametric, shelving, and notch filters boost or cut specific frequency bands to sculpt tonal balance and remove problem resonances.
*   **2.E.8 Distortion & Saturation Circuits:** Clipping, waveshaping, and tape/tube emulation add harmonic content and perceived loudness through controlled nonlinear signal processing.
*   **2.E.9 Sidechain & Ducking:** Trigger signal momentarily reduces target signal level, creating rhythmic pumping effect or clearing frequency space for kick/vocal clarity.
*   **2.E.10 Modular Signal Routing & CV:** Control voltage patch-based routing enables non-linear, experimental signal flow between oscillators, filters, and modulation sources freely.

### 2.F Acoustic Instrumentation & Texturing
**Category Overview:** Physical instrument timbre and performance technique knowledge governing orchestration choices, ensemble blend, and organic textural character across acoustic sound sources.

*   **2.F.1 String Family Timbre & Bowing:** Arco, pizzicato, col legno, sul ponticello bowing positions dramatically alter timbre from warm sustained tone to percussive scratch.
*   **2.F.2 Woodwind Overtone & Breath Control:** Embouchure and air-support shape overtone emphasis and dynamic range; breath noise and multiphonics add extended textural character.
*   **2.F.3 Brass Harmonic Series & Muting:** Valve/slide combinations access harmonic series partials; straight/cup/harmon mutes alter timbre from bright to nasal, distant character.
*   **2.F.4 Percussion Resonance & Damping:** Membrane tension, material composition, and damping technique control sustain, pitch definition, and attack transient sharpness across percussion families.
*   **2.F.5 Piano Action & Pedaling Nuance:** Hammer velocity curve, sustain/una-corda/sostenuto pedal combinations shape resonance, blur, and dynamic articulation range of struck strings.
*   **2.F.6 Extended/Prepared Techniques:** Objects placed on strings, unconventional embouchure, or body percussion extend traditional instrument vocabulary into novel timbral territory.
*   **2.F.7 Ensemble Blend & Orchestration Balance:** Doubling, register spacing, and dynamic layering achieve homogenous blend or intentional textural contrast across instrument families.
*   **2.F.8 Solo vs Section Voicing:** Single instrument exposes individual timbre and expressive nuance; unison sections create thickened, chorused, homogenized collective timbre.
*   **2.F.9 Cultural/Ethnic Instrument Timbres:** Non-Western instruments (sitar, koto, duduk, kalimba) carry distinct overtone structures and tuning systems, adding specific cultural sonic identity.
*   **2.F.10 Articulation & Dynamic Notation:** Staccato, legato, marcato, and dynamic markings (pp to ff) instruct performance nuance shaping phrase-level expressive character.

### 2.H Arrangement & Structural Archetypes
**Category Overview:** Compositional form and structural templates governing how musical sections are sequenced, developed, and paced to create satisfying large-scale narrative and momentum.

*   **2.H.1 Verse-Chorus-Bridge Form:** Repeating verse-chorus alternation with contrasting bridge section provides familiar popular music structural template balancing repetition and variation.
*   **2.H.2 Through-Composed Structures:** Continuously evolving non-repeating musical form without recurring sections, common in film scoring and progressive composition for narrative flow.
*   **2.H.3 AABA Song Form:** Classic 32-bar popular song structure with two A sections, contrasting B bridge, and returning A section resolution.
*   **2.H.4 Buildup & Drop Architecture:** Tension-accumulating rising elements (filter sweep, riser, snare roll) release into high-energy drop section, electronic music structural staple.
*   **2.H.5 Call-and-Response Structuring:** Alternating phrase exchange between voices or instrument groups creates conversational structural dialogue and antiphonal musical interaction.
*   **2.H.6 Instrumentation Density Curve:** Strategic addition/subtraction of instrumental layers across song timeline creates arc of intensity and structural momentum build.
*   **2.H.7 Intro/Outro Framing Devices:** Establishing sonic identity at entry and providing satisfying closure or fade at conclusion frames overall structural experience.
*   **2.H.8 Variation & Development Technique:** Thematic material altered through rhythm, harmony, or orchestration change while retaining recognizable core identity across sections.
*   **2.H.9 Tension-Release Cyclical Pacing:** Alternating harmonic/dynamic tension buildup and resolution across structural sections creates satisfying macro-level emotional pacing arc.
*   **2.H.10 Cinematic Cue Structural Timing:** Score structure synced to picture edit points, hit points, and emotional beats rather than independent musical form logic.

*   **2.I.2 Thematic Transformation & Variation:** Core motif altered in tempo, mode, orchestration, or harmony to reflect narrative/emotional evolution while preserving recognizable identity.

### 2.J Mixing & Mastering Reference Standards
**Category Overview:** Technical finalization processes governing loudness, spectral balance, and platform compatibility, ensuring translated, competitive, and professionally standardized final audio delivery.

*   **2.J.1 Gain Staging & Headroom Management:** Maintaining appropriate signal level through each processing stage prevents clipping while preserving sufficient headroom for mastering.
*   **2.J.2 Frequency Spectrum Balance:** Even distribution across low, mid, high bands prevents muddiness or harshness; reference spectrum analysis guides tonal balance decisions.
*   **2.J.3 Stereo Bus Processing:** Master bus compression, EQ, and saturation glue individual elements into cohesive final stereo mix with unified tonal character.
*   **2.J.4 LUFS Loudness Standards:** Platform-specific integrated loudness targets (streaming -14 LUFS, broadcast -23 LUFS) determine final mastering loudness normalization compliance.
*   **2.J.5 Reference Track Comparison Method:** A/B comparison against professionally mastered commercial reference tracks calibrates tonal balance and loudness perception during mix decisions.
*   **2.J.6 Multiband Compression:** Frequency-split dynamic control independently manages compression per band, allowing targeted dynamic control without full-spectrum pumping artifacts.
*   **2.J.7 Stem Mixing & Print Organization:** Grouped instrument category submixes (drums, bass, vocals) enable efficient parallel processing and simplified final mix balance.
*   **2.J.8 Dithering & Bit-Depth Conversion:** Noise-shaped dither added when reducing bit depth prevents quantization distortion, preserving perceived resolution at lower final bit depth.
*   **2.J.9 Platform-Specific Delivery Specs:** Streaming, broadcast, cinema, and game audio each require distinct format, loudness, and codec delivery specification compliance.
*   **2.J.10 A/B Translation Testing:** Verifying mix consistency across multiple playback systems (studio monitors, headphones, car, phone speaker) ensures broad listening-context translation.
