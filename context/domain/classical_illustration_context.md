### 2.A Dynamic Symmetry & Compositional Mathematics
**Category Overview:** Mathematical and geometric composition systems governing balance, focal placement, and visual flow that produce harmonious, intentional, and structurally sound image arrangement.

*   **2.A.1 Golden Ratio & Phi Grid:** Phi (1.618) proportional divisions and Fibonacci spiral guide focal placement and division for naturally harmonious compositional balance.
*   **2.A.2 Root Rectangles:** Dynamic symmetry rectangles (√2, √3, √5) generate reciprocal diagonals and division points for mathematically coherent proportional composition.
*   **2.A.3 Rule of Thirds:** Dividing frame into ninths; placing subjects on grid intersections creates balanced, engaging composition avoiding static central placement.
*   **2.A.4 Rabatment & Armature:** Folding square from short side and diagonal armature lines establish structural framework for element placement within rectangle.
*   **2.A.5 Focal Point Placement:** Directing eye to primary subject via contrast, isolation, convergence, and position; single dominant focus with subordinate secondary interest.
*   **2.A.6 Visual Weight Balance:** Distributing perceived mass (size, value, color, detail) for stable or dynamic equilibrium; balancing large quiet against small intense.
*   **2.A.7 Directional Lines & Flow:** Implied and actual lines guide eye movement through composition, creating rhythm, momentum, and controlled viewing path sequence.
*   **2.A.8 Notan & Mass Distribution:** Light-dark shape design as flat abstract pattern; strong notan ensures compositional readability independent of detail rendering.
*   **2.A.9 Radial & Grid Systems:** Compositional organization via radiating lines from central point or modular grid; structure imposes order and rhythmic repetition.
*   **2.A.10 Cropping & Frame Ratio:** Aspect ratio and edge cropping determine tension, emphasis, and inclusion; frame boundary actively shapes compositional relationship.

### 2.B Light Physics, Value Scales, & Form Rendering
**Category Overview:** Light-behavior and tonal-modeling principles governing how illumination reveals three-dimensional form through value, shadow, and highlight across rendered surfaces.

*   **2.B.1 Value Scale & Key:** Nine-step gray gradient from white to black; high-key (light) vs low-key (dark) tonal range sets mood.
*   **2.B.2 Light Logic & Terminator:** Form shadow begins at terminator where surface turns from light; light direction and angle govern shadow-shape logic consistently.
*   **2.B.3 Core Shadow & Occlusion:** Darkest form-shadow band opposite light source; occlusion shadow deepest where surfaces meet, blocking ambient bounce entirely.
*   **2.B.4 Reflected & Bounce Light:** Secondary illumination bouncing from adjacent surfaces lightens shadow interiors; reflected light never exceeds direct-light value on form.
*   **2.B.5 Cast Shadow Geometry:** Shadow projected onto surfaces follows light-ray geometry; shape, sharpness, and length depend on light size, distance, angle.
*   **2.B.6 Specular Highlight:** Direct light reflection appearing as brightest point; highlight position, size, and sharpness reveal surface glossiness and light character.
*   **2.B.7 Form Modeling Planes:** Breaking form into simplified light, halftone, and shadow planes; plane orientation relative to light determines value assignment.
*   **2.B.8 Chiaroscuro & Tenebrism:** Dramatic light-dark contrast modeling form; tenebrism pushes extreme darkness with selective spotlight illumination for theatrical effect.
*   **2.B.9 Local Value vs Light Value:** Object's inherent value (local) modified by illumination; light-affected value combines material tone and light intensity interaction.
*   **2.B.10 Ambient Occlusion:** Contact shadows in crevices and surface junctions where ambient light cannot penetrate; adds grounding and dimensional depth realism.

### 2.C Color Systems, Harmonies, & Gamut Masking
**Category Overview:** Color-theory frameworks governing hue relationships, temperature, and palette control that produce harmonious, expressive, and perceptually coherent color across images.

*   **2.C.1 Hue-Value-Chroma Model:** Munsell three-dimensional color space; independent control of hue (color), value (lightness), chroma (saturation) enables precise color specification.
*   **2.C.2 Color Temperature:** Warm (red-orange-yellow) vs cool (blue-green-violet) perception; temperature relationships create depth, mood, and light-source characterization.
*   **2.C.3 Complementary & Analogous:** Opposite-wheel complementary pairs maximize contrast/vibration; adjacent analogous colors create harmonious, unified, low-tension palette relationships.
*   **2.C.4 Gamut Masking:** Restricting palette to selected wheel region ensures color harmony; shaped mask limits available hue-chroma combinations for cohesion.
*   **2.C.5 Color Constancy & Adaptation:** Perception adjusts local color under varying light; understanding adaptation enables convincing light-color rendering and believable illumination shifts.
*   **2.C.6 Atmospheric Color Shift:** Distant objects shift cooler, lower-chroma, and toward atmospheric hue; color depth cue reinforces spatial recession convincingly.
*   **2.C.7 Simultaneous Contrast:** Adjacent colors influence mutual perception; complementary neighbors intensify, similar neighbors neutralize; context alters apparent hue and value.
*   **2.C.8 Limited Palette Strategy:** Restricting to few pigments (primary triad plus white) ensures harmony and forces mixing skill and unified color logic.
*   **2.C.9 Color Mixing (Subtractive):** Pigment mixing subtracts wavelengths; understanding bias, transparency, and mixing behavior predicts resulting hue, chroma, and muddiness avoidance.
*   **2.C.10 Mood & Emotional Color:** Palette choice evokes psychological response; warm energizes, cool calms, desaturation somber, high-chroma vibrant; color as emotional language.

### 2.D Human Anatomy, Kinesiology, & Proportions
**Category Overview:** Anatomical and biomechanical knowledge governing accurate figure construction, believable movement, and proportional integrity across body types, poses, and dynamic action.

*   **2.D.1 Skeletal Landmarks:** Bony surface points (clavicle, iliac crest, patella) anchor proportion and form; skeleton provides underlying structural framework for figure.
*   **2.D.2 Muscle Groups & Insertion:** Major muscle masses, origin-insertion points, and function; surface anatomy reflects underlying musculature affecting form and movement.
*   **2.D.3 Canonical Proportion:** Head-count systems (7.5-8 heads ideal); proportional ratios of limb, torso, and feature govern believable figure construction.
*   **2.D.4 Gesture & Line of Action:** Single flowing line capturing pose energy and rhythm; gesture prioritizes movement essence over anatomical detail initially.
*   **2.D.5 Contrapposto & Weight:** Asymmetric weight distribution shifting hips and shoulders in opposition creates natural, dynamic standing pose with believable balance.
*   **2.D.6 Foreshortening:** Limbs projecting toward viewer compress in apparent length; overlapping forms and diminished proportion convey depth along viewing axis.
*   **2.D.7 Facial Anatomy & Planes:** Skull structure, feature placement (Loomis method), and planar facial breakdown govern accurate portrait construction and likeness.
*   **2.D.8 Hands & Feet Construction:** Complex articulated forms simplified into boxes and wedges; proportional and structural logic tames notoriously difficult extremities.
*   **2.D.9 Joint Mechanics & Range:** Articulation limits and rotation axes at shoulder, elbow, hip, knee; joint mechanics constrain believable pose possibility.
*   **2.D.10 Age & Body Type Variation:** Proportional and structural shifts across age, sex, and somatotype; variation in fat, muscle, and bone prominence distinguishes figures.

### 2.E Perspective Geometry & Spatial Depth
**Category Overview:** Geometric projection systems governing spatial recession, vanishing-point convergence, and dimensional illusion that produce convincing depth on two-dimensional surfaces.

*   **2.E.1 One/Two/Three-Point Perspective:** Parallel lines converge to one, two, or three vanishing points; point count determines viewing angle and spatial dramatic effect.
*   **2.E.2 Horizon & Eye Level:** Horizon line marks viewer eye height; placement determines vantage (worm's-eye low, bird's-eye high) and compositional spatial relationship.
*   **2.E.3 Vanishing Points:** Convergence points where parallel receding lines meet on horizon; VP placement governs perspective geometry and object orientation.
*   **2.E.4 Foreshortening & Convergence:** Receding parallel edges appear to converge and compress; foreshortening rate depends on viewing angle and object orientation to picture plane.
*   **2.E.5 Ellipse & Circle Projection:** Circles in perspective render as ellipses; minor-axis alignment and degree of opening depend on viewing angle relative to circle plane.
*   **2.E.6 Curvilinear Perspective:** Wide-angle five-point fisheye projection curves straight lines; approximates human peripheral vision and extreme field-of-view spatial rendering.
*   **2.E.7 Scale & Diminution:** Objects diminish in apparent size with distance proportionally; consistent diminution rate establishes believable spatial scale relationship.
*   **2.E.8 Overlap & Occlusion Depth:** Nearer objects overlap and obscure farther ones; occlusion is the strongest, most immediate depth-ordering cue available.
*   **2.E.9 Grid & Floor Projection:** Perspective grid establishes ground plane for accurate object placement, scale, and spatial relationship measurement within scene.
*   **2.E.10 Isometric & Axonometric:** Parallel projection without convergence maintains true scale; technical, game, and diagram applications favor non-perspectival dimensional representation.

### 2.F Edge Control & Visual Focus Mechanics
**Category Overview:** Edge-quality manipulation systems governing focus direction, depth cueing, and rendering restraint that guide viewer attention and create dimensional atmospheric depth.

*   **2.F.1 Hard vs Soft Edges:** Sharp edges advance and attract focus; soft edges recede and relax; edge quality directs visual attention hierarchy.
*   **2.F.2 Lost & Found Edges:** Selectively dissolving and defining boundaries where value/color merge; lost edges create mystery, integration, and eye-movement flow.
*   **2.F.3 Focal Contrast Hierarchy:** Maximum edge sharpness and value contrast at focal point; diminishing contrast toward periphery establishes clear attention priority.
*   **2.F.4 Depth of Field Emulation:** Selective focus mimicking lens optics; sharp focal plane with progressive blur toward foreground and background depth extremes.
*   **2.F.5 Edge as Depth Cue:** Softer edges recede into distance; edge sharpness gradient reinforces spatial depth alongside atmospheric and scale cues.
*   **2.F.6 Rendering Restraint:** Strategic under-rendering of secondary areas prevents visual competition; restraint concentrates finish where narrative and focus demand.
*   **2.F.7 Detail Concentration:** Clustering high detail at focal area draws eye; sparse peripheral treatment supports hierarchy and prevents overwhelming uniformity.
*   **2.F.8 Selective Sharpness:** Deliberately sharpening only key edges while softening rest; controls read speed and directs perception intentionally through contrast.
*   **2.F.9 Silhouette Read:** Clear, readable outer contour communicates subject instantly; strong silhouette ensures recognition independent of internal detail rendering quality.
*   **2.F.10 Edge Variety Rhythm:** Alternating hard, firm, soft, and lost edges creates visual rhythm and interest; monotonous edge treatment flattens and bores.

### 2.G Traditional Media Chemistry & Emulation
**Category Overview:** Physical art-material behavior and properties governing authentic medium emulation, from pigment chemistry to substrate interaction across traditional painting and drawing media.

*   **2.G.1 Oil Paint Behavior:** Slow-drying, blendable, layerable pigment in oil binder; fat-over-lean rule, glazing, and impasto enable extended working and richness.
*   **2.G.2 Watercolor & Transparency:** Transparent pigment washes on paper; luminosity from white paper showing through, wet-on-wet blooms, and irreversible flowing behavior.
*   **2.G.3 Gouache Opacity:** Opaque water-based paint enabling coverage and correction; matte finish, reworkable, favored for flat color and illustration applications.
*   **2.G.4 Graphite & Charcoal:** Dry drawing media; graphite ranges hard-light to soft-dark, charcoal rich-black and smudgeable, both erasable and layerable tonally.
*   **2.G.5 Ink & Line Media:** Permanent flowing pigment for line and wash; brush, pen, and nib produce variable line weight and expressive mark.
*   **2.G.6 Pastel & Chalk:** Dry pigment sticks blending directly on textured surface; soft pastel rich and blendable, hard pastel/chalk precise and linear.
*   **2.G.7 Fresco & Tempera:** Pigment in wet plaster (fresco) or egg-yolk binder (tempera); fast-setting, luminous, durable historical painting techniques.
*   **2.G.8 Impasto & Texture:** Thick paint application retaining brush/knife marks; physical surface relief catches light, adding dimensional texture and gestural energy.
*   **2.G.9 Medium & Binder Chemistry:** Pigment-binder ratio, drying oils, solvents, and additives govern viscosity, drying time, finish, and paint-handling behavior.
*   **2.G.10 Substrate & Ground Preparation:** Canvas, panel, or paper prepared with gesso/sizing; substrate texture and absorbency affect paint adhesion and appearance.

### 2.H Texture, Material, & Surface Reflection Logic
**Category Overview:** Surface-appearance principles governing how different materials reflect, absorb, and transmit light, enabling convincing rendering of metal, skin, fabric, glass, and organic surfaces.

*   **2.H.1 Reflectance & Albedo:** Surface base brightness and proportion of light reflected; albedo determines local value independent of illumination and material identity.
*   **2.H.2 Specular vs Diffuse Surface:** Glossy surfaces produce sharp specular highlights; matte surfaces scatter diffuse reflection; ratio defines perceived material glossiness.
*   **2.H.3 Metal Rendering:** High-contrast reflection with sharp highlights, dark cores, and environmental color reflection; metals reflect surroundings rather than showing local color.
*   **2.H.4 Skin & Subsurface Scatter:** Light penetrating and diffusing beneath translucent skin creates warm glow; subsurface scattering softens shadows and reddens thin areas.
*   **2.H.5 Fabric & Cloth Behavior:** Drape, fold, and weave determine light interaction; matte cotton, sheen silk, and structured wool each render distinctly.
*   **2.H.6 Glass & Transparency:** Refraction, transmission, and reflection combine; transparent materials show background distorted plus surface reflection and edge caustics.
*   **2.H.7 Rough vs Smooth Texture:** Surface microstructure scatters (rough) or focuses (smooth) reflection; roughness controls highlight size, sharpness, and diffusion breadth.
*   **2.H.8 Wetness & Sheen:** Water film increases specularity and darkens local value; wet surfaces gain sharp highlights and enhanced reflection intensity.
*   **2.H.9 Fresnel & Grazing Angle:** Reflectance increases at grazing viewing angles; edges and glancing surfaces appear more reflective than surfaces facing viewer directly.
*   **2.H.10 Material Weathering:** Age, wear, dirt, and damage alter surface; scratches, patina, rust, and grime add history and material believability to rendering.

### 2.I Stylistic Line Dynamics & Structural Draftsmanship
**Category Overview:** Line-based drawing systems governing mark quality, constructive structure, and draftsmanship discipline that produce confident, expressive, and structurally accurate drawing.

*   **2.I.1 Line Weight Variation:** Modulating line thickness for emphasis, depth, and weight; heavy lines advance and ground, thin lines recede and delicate.
*   **2.I.2 Contour & Cross-Contour:** Outer edge contour defines silhouette; cross-contour lines wrap form describing surface volume and dimensional curvature effectively.
*   **2.I.3 Gesture vs Structural Line:** Fluid gesture captures movement and energy; structural line defines constructed form and volume; both balanced for lively accuracy.
*   **2.I.4 Hatching & Crosshatching:** Parallel and layered line groups build value and form; hatch direction, density, and spacing control tone and surface description.
*   **2.I.5 Line Economy:** Communicating maximum information with minimum marks; confident economical line reads clearly, avoiding tentative overworked scribbling and clutter.
*   **2.I.6 Constructive Drawing:** Building form from simple geometric primitives (spheres, boxes, cylinders); construction underpins accurate proportion, perspective, and dimensional believability.
*   **2.I.7 Sight-Size Measuring:** Comparing observed proportion and angle to drawing at matching scale; measurement technique ensures observational accuracy and proportion fidelity.
*   **2.I.8 Rhythm & Flow Lines:** Connected sweeping lines linking forms create visual rhythm and movement; flow guides eye and unifies composition dynamically.
*   **2.I.9 Calligraphic Mark:** Expressive brush/pen stroke with inherent variation and energy; calligraphic quality prizes gesture, spontaneity, and confident directional movement.
*   **2.I.10 Block-In & Refinement:** Establishing large shapes and proportions first, progressively refining detail; general-to-specific workflow prevents premature detail and proportion error.

### 2.J Atmospheric & Environmental Perspective Theory
**Category Overview:** Atmospheric-depth systems governing how distance affects value, chroma, and edge through air, producing convincing spatial recession and environmental scale illusion.

*   **2.J.1 Aerial Perspective Fade:** Distant objects lose contrast and detail, shifting toward atmospheric color; air density scatters light, softening distant elements.
*   **2.J.2 Value Compression by Distance:** Tonal range narrows toward midtone with distance; far objects lose dark-light extremes, converging on atmospheric value.
*   **2.J.3 Chroma Reduction by Depth:** Color saturation decreases with distance as atmosphere neutralizes; foreground vivid, background muted; chroma gradient reinforces spatial recession.
*   **2.J.4 Warm-Cool Depth Shift:** Distant elements cool toward blue-gray atmospheric hue; foreground retains warmth; temperature gradient enhances perceived spatial depth strongly.
*   **2.J.5 Edge Softening by Distance:** Distant edges blur as atmosphere diffuses; softening gradient from sharp foreground to hazy background reinforces recession.
*   **2.J.6 Atmospheric Scattering:** Particulate and moisture scatter shorter wavelengths (blue); scattering explains sky color, distant blue shift, and haze physics.
*   **2.J.7 Depth Layering Planes:** Organizing scene into distinct foreground, midground, background value/chroma bands; layered planes create clear, readable spatial depth structure.
*   **2.J.8 Contrast Falloff:** Progressive reduction of value and color contrast toward distance; strongest contrast reserved for foreground focal emphasis and depth.
*   **2.J.9 Light Diffusion & Haze:** Fog, mist, and haze scatter light uniformly, flattening form and reducing visibility; density controls atmospheric mood and depth.
*   **2.J.10 Sky-Ground Value Relationship:** Sky typically lightest value; ground-plane and vertical-object values calibrated relative to sky establish consistent lighting and depth logic.
