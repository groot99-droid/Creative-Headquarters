### 2.A Geopolitical Mechanics & Power Distributions
**Category Overview:** Systems governing sovereignty, governance, and inter-polity power relations that produce believable political dynamics, conflict, and institutional stability across constructed worlds.

*   **2.A.1 Sovereignty & Borders:** Territorial control legitimacy, contested frontiers, buffer zones, and border-drawing logic (natural, ethnic, imposed) shape geopolitical friction and identity.
*   **2.A.2 Governance Systems:** Monarchy, republic, oligarchy, theocracy, technocracy each distribute authority differently; structure determines decision-making, legitimacy source, and power concentration.
*   **2.A.3 Power Balance & Hegemony:** Multipolar, bipolar, unipolar configurations; hegemonic stability vs balance-of-power dynamics govern alliance formation and systemic conflict likelihood.
*   **2.A.4 Diplomacy & Treaties:** Formal agreements, alliances, and negotiation mechanisms; treaty enforcement depends on power asymmetry, mutual interest, and credible commitment.
*   **2.A.5 Warfare & Deterrence:** Military capability, doctrine, and deterrence logic; force projection, defensive posture, and threat credibility shape conflict initiation calculus.
*   **2.A.6 Succession & Legitimacy:** Hereditary, elective, or conquest-based power transfer; succession crises and legitimacy contests drive instability and civil conflict.
*   **2.A.7 Espionage & Intelligence:** Covert information gathering, counterintelligence, and clandestine operations shape strategic advantage, mistrust, and asymmetric power between polities.
*   **2.A.8 Trade Blocs & Sanctions:** Economic alliance formation and coercive economic pressure as non-military statecraft tools shaping alignment, dependency, and leverage.
*   **2.A.9 Insurgency & Rebellion:** Asymmetric resistance against established power; grievance, opportunity, and resource mobilization determine insurgency emergence and sustainability logic.
*   **2.A.10 Territorial Resource Contest:** Competition over strategic geography, chokepoints, and resource-rich territory drives expansionism, conflict, and geopolitical maneuvering between powers.

### 2.B Macro & Micro Socio-Economics
**Category Overview:** Economic systems at societal and individual scale governing value exchange, class dynamics, and production that ground world material realism and social structure.

*   **2.B.1 Currency & Exchange Systems:** Commodity, fiat, or barter-based value mediums; monetary standards, coinage, and exchange mechanisms enable trade and store value.
*   **2.B.2 Class Stratification:** Hierarchical social layers based on wealth, birth, or function; mobility rates and class rigidity shape social tension and identity.
*   **2.B.3 Labor & Production Modes:** Slavery, feudal, wage, or guild labor organization; production relations determine wealth distribution and class relationship structure.
*   **2.B.4 Supply & Demand Dynamics:** Price determined by scarcity-desire equilibrium; shortage, surplus, and speculation drive market behavior and economic instability cycles.
*   **2.B.5 Trade Routes & Logistics:** Physical goods-movement networks; route control, transport cost, and chokepoint access shape wealth flow and settlement patterns.
*   **2.B.6 Taxation & Redistribution:** Revenue extraction methods (tribute, tariff, income) fund institutions; redistribution level affects inequality and social contract stability.
*   **2.B.7 Market vs Command Economy:** Decentralized price-signal allocation vs centralized planned distribution; each produces distinct efficiency, equity, and control tradeoffs.
*   **2.B.8 Debt & Credit Systems:** Lending, interest, and obligation mechanisms enable investment but generate debt bondage, financial crisis, and creditor-debtor power dynamics.
*   **2.B.9 Scarcity & Abundance Cycles:** Boom-bust economic oscillation from harvest, resource, or market fluctuation drives migration, conflict, and social upheaval periodically.
*   **2.B.10 Informal & Black Markets:** Unregulated shadow economies emerge around prohibition, scarcity, and taxation; parallel systems undermine or supplement official economic structure.

### 2.C Resource Distribution & Thermodynamic Constraints
**Category Overview:** Physical resource availability and energy-conservation constraints governing sustainability, carrying capacity, and material limits that ground world plausibility in thermodynamic reality.

*   **2.C.1 Energy Sources & Density:** Fuel type (biomass, fossil, nuclear, exotic) determines energy density, availability, and technological ceiling; EROEI governs civilizational capability.
*   **2.C.2 Water & Hydrological Cycles:** Freshwater availability, precipitation patterns, and watershed geography determine settlement viability, agriculture, and conflict over scarce hydrological resources.
*   **2.C.3 Food Systems & Caloric Load:** Agricultural yield, caloric surplus, and food storage determine population ceiling, urbanization capacity, and famine vulnerability thresholds.
*   **2.C.4 Material & Mineral Extraction:** Ore, timber, and raw material deposits, extraction difficulty, and depletion rates shape industry, trade, and territorial value.
*   **2.C.5 Carrying Capacity:** Maximum sustainable population given resource base; overshoot triggers collapse, migration, or technological intensification to raise ceiling.
*   **2.C.6 Entropy & Waste Management:** Byproduct accumulation, pollution, and waste-disposal capacity constrain sustainable activity; entropy imposes irreducible inefficiency on all systems.
*   **2.C.7 Renewable vs Finite Resources:** Regenerating (forest, fish) vs depleting (ore, fossil) resource distinction determines long-term sustainability and extraction strategy consequences.
*   **2.C.8 Distribution Networks:** Physical infrastructure moving resources from source to consumption; network efficiency, bottlenecks, and vulnerability shape economic and strategic reality.
*   **2.C.9 Resource Curse Dynamics:** Resource abundance paradoxically breeds corruption, conflict, and economic distortion; rent-seeking undermines diversified development and institutional health.
*   **2.C.10 Conservation Laws & Limits:** Energy/matter cannot be created or destroyed; magic and technology must obey or explicitly define exceptions to thermodynamic constraint.

### 2.D Anthropological Evolution & Cultural Heritage
**Category Overview:** Cultural systems governing kinship, ritual, and tradition that produce believable, dimensional societies with authentic internal logic and heritage continuity.

*   **2.D.1 Kinship & Family Structure:** Descent systems (patrilineal, matrilineal, bilateral), marriage patterns, and household organization structure social obligation, inheritance, and identity.
*   **2.D.2 Ritual & Rites of Passage:** Ceremonies marking life transitions (birth, adulthood, marriage, death) reinforce social bonds, values, and communal identity across generations.
*   **2.D.3 Myth & Cosmology:** Origin stories, pantheons, and worldview frameworks explain existence, justify social order, and encode cultural values and moral logic.
*   **2.D.4 Taboo & Social Norms:** Prohibited behaviors and unwritten conduct rules regulate society; violation consequences and taboo logic reveal cultural priorities and fears.
*   **2.D.5 Material Culture & Craft:** Tools, clothing, art, and craft traditions embody cultural aesthetics, technology, and identity; artifacts encode social meaning and status.
*   **2.D.6 Migration & Diaspora:** Population movement, displacement, and settlement create cultural mixing, identity preservation tension, and hybrid diasporic community formation dynamics.
*   **2.D.7 Oral vs Written Tradition:** Knowledge transmission mode (memorized oral vs recorded text) shapes cultural memory, authority, literacy distribution, and information permanence.
*   **2.D.8 Cultural Diffusion & Syncretism:** Contact between cultures spreads ideas, technology, and practices; syncretism blends traditions into novel hybrid cultural forms.
*   **2.D.9 Status & Prestige Systems:** Honor, reputation, and social ranking mechanisms; prestige earned through wealth, achievement, birth, or piety governs social competition.
*   **2.D.10 Foodways & Cuisine Identity:** Diet, preparation, and commensality practices express cultural identity, class, religion, and geography; food as powerful heritage marker.

### 2.E Historical Timelines & Continuity Mapping
**Category Overview:** Chronological architecture and causal-consistency systems governing world history depth, event causality, and continuity integrity across extended temporal narrative scope.

*   **2.E.1 Chronology & Dating Systems:** Calendar systems, era markers, and reckoning methods provide temporal framework; dating logic anchors events in coherent historical sequence.
*   **2.E.2 Cause-Effect Event Chains:** Historical events linked by plausible causality; consequence propagation creates layered, interconnected history rather than isolated arbitrary occurrences.
*   **2.E.3 Golden Ages & Collapses:** Cyclical rise-and-fall of civilizations; prosperity peaks followed by decline from overextension, resource depletion, or systemic fragility.
*   **2.E.4 Foundational Myths & Origins:** Formative founding narratives (real or mythologized) legitimize institutions, identity, and territorial claims across a culture's self-understanding.
*   **2.E.5 Dynastic & Regime Change:** Power transitions between ruling lineages or systems; succession, revolution, and conquest reshape political and social landscape periodically.
*   **2.E.6 Technological Epoch Shifts:** Innovation-driven era transitions (stone-bronze-iron equivalents) restructure warfare, economy, and society through capability transformation and disruption.
*   **2.E.7 Continuity Consistency Audit:** Cross-referencing established facts, timelines, and cause-effect to prevent contradiction; maintaining internal logical coherence across accumulated lore.
*   **2.E.8 Alternate History Divergence:** Counterfactual branching from established point; single altered variable propagates cascading differences producing divergent yet plausible timeline.
*   **2.E.9 Recorded vs Lost History:** Distinction between documented, mythologized, and forgotten past; historical gaps and unreliable records create mystery and interpretive ambiguity.
*   **2.E.10 Cyclical vs Linear Time Models:** Cultural conception of time as repeating cycle vs progressive line shapes worldview, prophecy, and historical interpretation frameworks.

### 2.F Architectural Eras & Structural Philosophies
**Category Overview:** Built-environment systems governing construction logic, stylistic evolution, and structural philosophy that ground world physicality in material, climatic, and cultural authenticity.

*   **2.F.1 Material & Construction Method:** Available materials (stone, timber, brick, composite) and joining techniques determine structural possibility, form vocabulary, and building longevity.
*   **2.F.2 Vernacular & Climate Response:** Local building traditions adapt to climate (thick walls for heat, steep roofs for snow), materials, and cultural need.
*   **2.F.3 Monumental & Sacred Structures:** Temples, palaces, and monuments express power, faith, and cultural ambition through scale, permanence, and symbolic architectural language.
*   **2.F.4 Urban Planning & Density:** Settlement layout (grid, organic, radial), density, and zoning reflect social organization, defense, commerce, and infrastructure logic.
*   **2.F.5 Load-Bearing & Structural Logic:** Arch, vault, dome, truss, and cantilever systems distribute structural forces; engineering capability determines achievable span and height.
*   **2.F.6 Ornamentation & Symbolism:** Decorative program encodes cultural meaning, status, and belief; ornament density and style signal era, wealth, and function.
*   **2.F.7 Defensive Architecture:** Fortification design (walls, moats, bastions) responds to siege technology; military architecture reflects threat environment and warfare evolution.
*   **2.F.8 Infrastructure & Utilities:** Water supply, sanitation, roads, and drainage systems enable urban scale; infrastructure sophistication reflects civilizational capability and organization.
*   **2.F.9 Ruins & Decay Layering:** Weathering, structural failure, and abandonment patterns; ruin state encodes history, prior function, and time depth into environment.
*   **2.F.10 Stylistic Era Signatures:** Recognizable formal vocabulary distinguishes architectural periods; style evolution reflects technology, values, and cultural exchange across time.

### 2.G Biome Ecosystems & Environmental Physics
**Category Overview:** Ecological and geophysical systems governing climate, terrain, and life-web dynamics that produce believable, self-consistent natural environments and their physical behavior.

*   **2.G.1 Climate Zones & Weather Systems:** Latitude, altitude, and geography determine temperature, precipitation, and seasonal patterns; atmospheric circulation drives predictable regional climate distribution.
*   **2.G.2 Flora & Vegetation Layers:** Plant communities stratified by canopy, understory, and ground cover; vegetation reflects climate, soil, and ecological succession stage.
*   **2.G.3 Fauna & Food Webs:** Trophic relationships linking producers, herbivores, and predators; energy flow, population dynamics, and niche partitioning structure ecosystem stability.
*   **2.G.4 Geology & Landform Genesis:** Tectonics, erosion, and deposition sculpt mountains, valleys, and plains; geological history explains terrain, minerals, and resource distribution.
*   **2.G.5 Hydrosphere & Ocean Currents:** Water circulation, thermohaline currents, and freshwater systems distribute heat, nutrients, and moisture, governing climate and marine ecology.
*   **2.G.6 Atmospheric Composition:** Gas mixture, pressure, and density determine breathability, sky color, weather intensity, and combustion; deviation from Earth-normal requires biological adaptation.
*   **2.G.7 Ecological Succession:** Predictable community change over time from pioneer to climax; disturbance resets succession, creating patchwork of ecological development stages.
*   **2.G.8 Biodiversity & Endemism:** Species richness and locally-unique organisms; isolation (islands, valleys) breeds endemism; biodiversity reflects habitat stability and geological age.
*   **2.G.9 Natural Disaster Cycles:** Earthquakes, floods, storms, and volcanism as recurring environmental forces shaping landscape, settlement risk, and cultural adaptation strategies.
*   **2.G.10 Human-Environment Interaction:** Anthropogenic landscape modification (agriculture, deforestation, irrigation) and environmental feedback shape sustainability, degradation, and coevolutionary human-ecology dynamics.

*   **2.H.1 Tech Level Consistency:** Uniform technological capability across society avoids anachronism; tech tier constrains warfare, economy, communication, and daily-life possibility coherently.
*   **2.H.2 Energy & Propulsion Systems:** Available power generation and movement technology (muscle, steam, electric, exotic) determines transport speed, industry scale, and reach.
*   **2.H.3 Communication & Information Tech:** Message-transmission speed and range (courier, signal, instantaneous) shapes governance scale, coordination capability, and information asymmetry dynamics.
*   **2.H.4 Medical & Biotech Capability:** Disease treatment, surgery, and life-extension technology affects mortality, population, warfare survivability, and demographic structure of society.
*   **2.H.5 Materials Science Limits:** Available material strength, weight, and properties constrain structural, tool, and weapon possibility; novel materials enable capability leaps.
*   **2.H.6 Magic-as-System Rules:** Consistent, rule-bound supernatural mechanics with defined cost, limit, and logic; hard magic enables plot problem-solving without deus-ex-machina.
*   **2.H.7 Automation & Computation:** Calculation, control, and labor-substitution technology; automation level restructures economy, labor, and cognitive-task capability across society.
*   **2.H.8 Scientific Method & Discovery:** Knowledge-generation approach (empirical, dogmatic, mystical) determines innovation rate, error correction, and relationship between belief and reality.
*   **2.H.9 Tech Diffusion & Access:** Uneven technology distribution across regions, classes, and factions creates power asymmetry, dependency, and conflict over capability access.
*   **2.H.10 Unintended Consequence Modeling:** Technology generates second-order effects (pollution, disruption, dependency); realistic worlds trace ripple consequences of capability introduction over time.

### 2.I Language, Lexicons, & Nomenclature Systems
**Category Overview:** Linguistic systems governing sound, structure, and naming that produce consistent, believable languages and nomenclature reinforcing cultural identity and world depth.

*   **2.I.1 Phonology & Sound Inventory:** Consonant-vowel repertoire and permitted sound combinations define language's auditory character; phonotactic rules govern syllable and word structure.
*   **2.I.2 Morphology & Word Formation:** Root, affix, and compounding rules build words; agglutinative, fusional, or isolating typology determines grammatical structure and complexity.
*   **2.I.3 Syntax & Grammar Rules:** Word-order (SVO, SOV), case marking, and agreement systems structure meaning; consistent grammar distinguishes constructed language from gibberish.
*   **2.I.4 Naming Conventions:** Systematic person, place, and thing naming logic reflecting phonology, culture, and meaning; consistent conventions create believable nomenclature cohesion.
*   **2.I.5 Writing Systems & Scripts:** Alphabetic, syllabic, or logographic scripts encode language visually; script form reflects material, culture, and historical development.
*   **2.I.6 Dialect & Register Variation:** Regional, class, and situational speech variation; dialect and formality register encode social identity, geography, and relationship dynamics.
*   **2.I.7 Etymology & Loanwords:** Word origins and borrowing between languages encode contact history; etymology reveals cultural exchange, conquest, and historical layering.
*   **2.I.8 Idiom & Figurative Language:** Culture-specific expressions, metaphors, and sayings encode worldview; idiom reflects environment, values, and shared collective experience.
*   **2.I.9 Language Evolution & Drift:** Sound change, semantic shift, and grammatical simplification over time; languages diverge into families through gradual accumulated change.
*   **2.I.10 Constructed Language Consistency:** Maintaining internal rule-coherence across vocabulary, grammar, and usage; consistency distinguishes believable conlang from arbitrary decorative placeholder text.
